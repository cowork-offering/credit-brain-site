# Delivery methodology — how the brain is built, phased, and governed

## Flagship-first, then reusable
The brain is proven and hardened on one flagship lender's estate, then becomes the reusable banking asset. What changes for the next lender is only the data foundation, the entitlements, and the policy. Every lender after the flagship inherits the hardened asset. Engagement N+1 starts with everything engagements 1 through N proved.

## Phasing — MVP-direct, then R1, then scale
- MVP-direct: route one slice through the brain on the existing estate. In commercial credit that slice is the credit memo (underwrite), the flagship agent, which is built and running. The move from today is to re-point the model to Bedrock and re-host the servers into AgentCore. No rewrites.
- R1: add the next agent beneath the same brain. Early Warning monitoring is next. Because the servers and connections already exist, the second and twentieth agents cost a fraction of the first.
- Scale: more agents, AgentCore Gateway, AgentCore Identity, and headless agents, all added under the same brain, never a competing design.

## Governance — propose, human gate, graduate
Nothing becomes institutional truth until a person approves it. Knowledge, rules, and agents are proposed, pass through a human curation gate with provenance tags and a full audit trail across capture, promotion, and retrieval, and then graduate into a versioned foundation cataloged for reuse (on Azure, in API Center). There is a human gate at every scope rung — practice, company, client, industry — and promoted copies keep their lineage. This human-gated promotion is the compounding moat: the brain gets measurably better every engagement.

## Non-functional intent
In-account inference with no training on the data, per-user entitlements so a person only ever reaches what they already can, a complete audit trail, grounding plus PII and denied-topic control via Guardrails, and scale to roughly 30,000 users on session-isolated, long-running runtime.
